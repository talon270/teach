## Chosen direction

The user selected **“The nuclear taboo versus military utility”**, originally phrased as: *If nuclear weapons have military uses, what has restrained their use?* The two assigned readings identified were:

1. Robert S. McNamara, “[The Military Role of Nuclear Weapons: Perceptions and Misperceptions](https://www.foreignaffairs.com/articles/1983-09-01/military-role-nuclear-weapons-perceptions-and-misperceptions),” *Foreign Affairs* 62, no. 1 (1983): 59–80 — Week 9.
2. Nina Tannenwald, “[The Nuclear Taboo: The United States and the Normative Basis of Nuclear Non-Use](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/international-organization/article/nuclear-taboo-the-united-states-and-the-normative-basis-of-nuclear-nonuse/EA04E0104A42C12FC785A70F301197CC),” *International Organization* 53, no. 3 (1999): 433–468 — Week 11.

**Important correction to the initial framing:** McNamara argues that nuclear weapons have no military purpose beyond deterring an opponent's nuclear use; his article addresses NATO's potential first use in a European war. Tannenwald argues that military calculations alone do not explain post-1945 non-use: a norm stigmatizing nuclear use also constrains decisions. Her article examines the U.S. record, including Hiroshima, Korea, Vietnam, and the 1991 Gulf War. Do not portray McNamara as arguing for nuclear battlefield use, or treat his NATO discussion and Tannenwald's Gulf War case as accounts of the same decision.

One provisional question was **“The taboo stops the bomb. What does it stop?”** Tannenwald's Gulf War section raises the possibility that stigma against nuclear weapons coexists with acceptance of destructive conventional warfare. This is a potential argument, not a thesis the user has approved. A narrower focus on NATO first use is also available within McNamara's reading.

## Visual and evidentiary cautions

- The protest photograph shows opposition to **missile deployment**; it does not prove a taboo against **wartime nuclear use**.
- The highway photograph shows abandoned vehicles; it does not document civilian infrastructure damage or establish casualties.
- The F-117 photograph shows a takeoff; it does not identify a target or the outcome of that mission.
- The Pershing photograph shows a missile stage being handled, not a launch or an armed warhead.
- The photos depict events related to the assigned readings, but **none is reproduced in the readings**. Analyse what each image actually shows and cite its archive caption.
