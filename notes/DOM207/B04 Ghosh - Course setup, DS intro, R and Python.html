Where the course starts

What data science is, what this course will (and won't) teach, and how to set up R and Python.

Jaideep Ghosh, Data Science using R and Python: Introduction (Lect-Intro-1, 26 slides; Lect-CourseIntro, 25 slides) and Introduction to R and Python (Lect-Intro-R-Python, 38 slides). School of Management and Entrepreneurship, Shiv Nadar University. DOM207, Monsoon 2026.

I1 Intro-1 in one breath. DS is the multidisciplinary study of data (maths, statistics, AI, computer engineering) to extract insights: what happened, why, what will happen, what to do. You need a love of data, not to be a coding geek or a statistician. A one-act play on 519 heads in 1,000 tosses shows that chance has a margin (about √1000 ≈ 32). The deck tours data examples (call-centre logs, stock data, clickstreams, tweets), the data scientist's many roles, data volumes (Google > 50 PB/day) and the KB-to-YB units. It then sorts data types: numerical (modelled by time series or regression), non-numerical (happiness, perception), and structured, unstructured and semi-structured data (email, XML).

CI CourseIntro in one breath. The course is not an R, Python, Excel, statistics or maths course. It teaches DS techniques, with R and Python learned along the way, then advanced methods through to LLMs, plus mini-projects (MPs) on real data. It is a 4-credit course with surprise tests, and all test and end-term questions come only from the lecture notes. Four HCL problems preview the methods: a gender pay gap (t-test), salary drivers (multiple regression), fraud detection (random forest) and website music (LLM).

IR Intro-R-Python in one breath. Make course folders. Install R from CRAN, then RStudio (R first or RStudio won't work), and use projects (.Rproj); Git is optional here. Packages are installed, loaded, unloaded and removed with install.packages(), require()/library(), detach() and remove.packages(). Python is a readable general-purpose language with several IDEs: PyCharm, IDLE, Spyder (via Anaconda), Jupyter, and the lecturer's favourite, Google Colab.

6

Statistical confusion: a one-act play

6Statistical Confusion – A One-Act Play
StudentSir, you told us the chance of getting a Head in a coin toss is ½ or just 50%. Well, I tossed a coin 1000 times and got 519 heads instead of 500. So, it doesn't work! TeacherPerhaps it's a biased coin. StudentSir, I'm pretty sure it was a perfectly good coin. TeacherWell. you forgot about the error margin, which goes as the square root of the # of tosses. In your case, it's about 32. StudentSo that means anything between 468 and 532 is good. TeacherYep. StudentIf I tried 10,000 times, would it be better? TeacherProbably.

outside slides√1000 = 31.6 ≈ 32, so 500 ± 32 = 468 to 532, and 519 is well inside. For a fair coin the SD of the head count is √(n·½·½) = √n / 2, so "±√n" is exactly ±2 SD, about a 95% band (simulated: 96% of fair-coin runs land in 468–532). With 10,000 tosses the margin grows to ±100 heads but shrinks as a share, from ±3.2% to ±1%; that is the sense in which it is "better". This play is the seed of B07's binomial and hypothesis testing. Try it in the lab.

Four real-life problems in DSA

13Now let's talk about four real-life problems in DSA14–16Problem 1 · Steps · Resolution17–19Problem 2 · Steps · Resolution20–22Problem 3 · Steps · Resolution23–25Problem 4 · Steps · Resolution

1 · Fair pay across genders sl. 14–16

HCL Management has given you the following project: their HR Department is keen to know if HCL has a fair salary distribution across employee genders (male, female), i.e. whether a female employee earns the same or very similar salary as a male employee with all similar or very comparable characteristics: tenure, educational qualification, industry experience, etc. You collect salary data from, say, 150 male and 150 female employees: average 88,000/month vs 84,000/month. Does this mean men earn more? No: the difference may have occurred purely by chance, depending on the sample. Show whether it is a significant (real) effect or just a chance effect.

→ the t-test (B08a)

2 · What drives salary? sl. 17–19

HR plans a competent salary package for all salaried employees. After consulting studies and experts, salary may depend on years of industry experience, educational qualification, gender, years in HCL, designation/position and age, but you don't know which are important. You need a model to test which factors matter most.

→ multiple regression analysis (B09–B10)

3 · E-commerce fraud sl. 20–22

HCL has a large client in the e-commerce business, where fraud detection is very important: when a customer places an order, you must assess the transaction in real time and classify it as legitimate or fraudulent. Look at a variety of customer data points (features) to make the final assessment: transaction time (is the purchase happening at an unusual hour?), IP location (does it match the user's historical account data?), order value (is the spending amount significantly higher than their average purchase?), device used (is the customer logging in from a new or unverified device?). The model (also called an algorithm) must aggregate many opinions (random combinations of features and data points), take a majority vote, and prevent overfitting ("not too much tinkering").

→ random forest, an advanced ML algorithm based on decision trees (DTs)

4 · Music for a website sl. 23–25

HCL wants a website marketing its innovative services to play nice thematic music when it opens, to give clients a good feeling; the music must not be of a single genre but combine several popular genres innovatively. You've decided mixing jazz and classical will produce a nice pleasing effect, so you want to infuse elements from both; you think this'll produce an innovative fusion piece that still feels grounded in both genres; "Jazz is also incredibly cool and can be used as a guide to help improvise!" A highly technical problem for advanced DSA techniques.

→ a large language model (LLM), a very advanced ML technique (end of course)

outside slidesProblem 1 is the same HCL fairness question the course file lists as the core problem → method example, and the sl. 15 trap ("does this mean men earn more? No") is exactly the B07/B08 idea of a chance difference. Strictly, music is generated by audio/music generative models; the slide groups them under LLMs.
