Do the three methods differ?

One ANOVA table in statsmodels, then the first step into regression.

Jaideep Ghosh, Analysis of Variance (ANOVA): "ANOVA in Python" (Lect-ANOVA-R-Python-1, 7 slides; Lect-ANOVA-R-Python-2, 7 slides) and Multiple Linear Regression (MLR) Analysis (Lect-MLR-Theory-1, 7 slides). School of Management and Entrepreneurship, Shiv Nadar University. DOM207, Monsoon 2026.

ANOVA in one breath. Both ANOVA decks teach one recipe. Fit ols('DV ~ C(IV)', data=…).fit() from statsmodels.formula.api, where C() marks the IV as categorical. Then pass the model to sm.stats.anova_lm(mdl, typ=1) (Type 1 = sequential sums of squares) and print the table. The worked example has nine students' scores under teaching methods A, B and C. The output gives F = 6.958 on (2, 6) df, p = 0.0273. The decks print this table without interpreting it; read at α = 0.05, it says the three methods' mean scores are not all equal. Deck 2 is deck 1 with three comments added and a fixed import line.

MLR-Theory-1 in one breath. MLR is "one of the most important of all top-down approaches" in DSA: hypothesise a relationship, then test it empirically. The DV is the outcome you decide about; the IVs are the predictors you think influence it; fix the SL at this stage. HCL wants to engineer a salary package, so hypothesise salary depends on education. The bivariate model y = β₀ + β₁x + ε becomes sal = β₀ + β₁edu + ε. β₀ is the salary at zero education ("does it make sense? You need to decide"), and β₁ is the salary rise per extra year of education.

import statsmodels.api as smImports the statsmodels library (sl. 4).
from statsmodels.formula.api import olsImports the OLS library for MLR (sl. 4). outside slidesols is one function: ordinary least squares with an R-style formula.
DV ~ C(IV)The formula defines the relationship between the DV and the IVs "in the formula" (A1; A2 drops "in the formula"). The C() function treats the IV as categorical.
data=datasetSpecifies your dataset. It must be a structured data format, such as a Pandas DataFrame, where the variables in the formula are columns in the dataset (sl. 5).
sm.stats.anova_lm(model, typ=1)Performs an ANOVA analysis on the fitted model (sl. 5). [model → mdl]
mdlThe fitted model created by the ols function.
typ=1Specifies the type of sum of squares to use in the ANOVA calculation. Type 1 is a sequential sum of squares, which evaluates each variable in the order it appears in the formula.

outside slidesWith only one IV, as here, Type 1, 2 and 3 give the same table; the order only matters with several IVs. .fit() estimates the model; without it, anova_lm has nothing to analyse. Writing Method without C() would also work for text labels A/B/C, but C() is essential when categories are coded as numbers (1, 2, 3), which would otherwise be treated as a numeric slope.

Example: scores under three teaching methods

6Example Code7Output
PythonA1/A2 sl. 6
# scores obtained by students in different methods of teaching
import pandas as pd
import statsmodels.api as sm
from statsmodels.formula.api import ols

# sample data
data = pd.DataFrame({
    'Score': [85, 89, 76, 71, 80, 78, 93, 95, 88],
    'Method': ['A', 'A', 'A', 'B', 'B', 'B', 'C', 'C', 'C']
})

mdl = ols('Score ~ C(Method)', data=data).fit()
anova_tab = sm.stats.anova_lm(mdl, typ=1)
print(anova_tab) # anova-tab is the results object

d <- data.frame(

  Method = factor(rep(c("A","B","C"), each = 3)))
summary(aov(Score ~ Method, data = d))

Reading the ANOVA table

outside slides
Hypotheses
H₀: μA = μB = μC (all teaching methods give the same mean score). H₁: at least one mean differs.
Group means
A 83.33, B 76.33, C 92.00; grand mean 83.89.
sum_sq
Between groups (C(Method)): 369.56 = 3 × [(83.33 − 83.89)² + (76.33 − 83.89)² + (92 − 83.89)²]. Within groups (Residual): 159.33, the spread of students around their own group's mean.
df
Groups − 1 = 2; observations − groups = 9 − 3 = 6.
mean_sq
sum_sq / df: 369.56/2 = 184.78 and 159.33/6 = 26.56.
F
Between-group MS ÷ within-group MS = 184.78 / 26.56 = 6.958. A large F means groups differ more than students within a group do.
PR(>F)
The p-value: 0.0273 < 0.05 → reject H₀; mean scores are not the same for all three methods. The NaNs simply mean the Residual row has no F or p.
Which differ?
ANOVA does not say. A Tukey HSD follow-up finds only B vs C significant (difference 15.67, adjusted p = 0.023).
Assumptions
Those of the t-test: independent observations, normal groups, equal variances (Levene p = 0.80 here). With 3 students per group the test has little power.

1–3slides

MLR in DSA, and the variables

1Multiple Linear Regression (MLR) Analysis2MLR in DSA3Variables in MLR
Ghosh · M1 sl. 2

"MLR is one of the most important of all top-down approaches to problem-solving in DSA." First, you hypothesize a relationship between variables. Then, you empirically test the relationship using well-defined statistical procedures.

DV sl. 3

Outcome / Response / Criterion (DV): your study variable, about which you need to make decisions.

IV sl. 3

Explanatory / Predictor / Regressor (IV): the variables you hypothesize will influence your DV.

NOTE (sl. 3): for empirical testing of your model, you need to fix a significance level (SL) right at this stage, before any data are analysed. This is the same rule as B08b sl. 15: choosing an SL to fit your result is cheating.

A specific application: salary at HCL

4A Specific Application
Case · M1 sl. 4

Suppose HCL wants to estimate a good salary package for its employees. What determines employee salary in HCL? How can a competent salary package be engineered? Suppose you hypothesize that employee salary is determined by educational qualification.

outside slidesSo salary is the DV and education the IV. With one IV the model is bivariate (sl. 5); adding more IVs (experience, gender, department) makes it multiple. Data Files/Salary.csv is probably the data for the later MLR decks; this deck does not use it.

5–6slides

Bivariate regression, and salary on education

5Bivariate Regression6Bivariate Regression for Salary

The ordinary bivariate regression model assesses a DV (y) in terms of only one IV (x) (sl. 5):

Bivariate model · M1 sl. 5y = β₀ + β₁x + ε

ε sl. 5

The error or disturbance term. It contains unobserved terms, not included in x.

β₀ sl. 5

The constant or intercept coefficient.

β₁ sl. 5

The slope coefficient.

Applied to HCL (sl. 6): the salary (sal) of an employee is determined by his/her years of education (edu):

Population model · M1 sl. 6sal = β₀ + β₁edu + ε
Predicted form · M1 sl. 6ŝal = β̂₀ + β̂₁edu  — β̂₀ and β̂₁ are the coefficients estimated from data

outside slidesHats mark estimates. The population β's are unknown; the β̂'s come from a sample (by OLS, the ols function of the ANOVA decks). The predicted form drops ε because its expected value is zero.

Interpretation of coefficients

7Interpretation of Coefficients

Reading β̂₀ and β̂₁

M1 sl. 7
β̂₀
The salary when the level of education is 0. "Does it make sense? You need to decide."
β̂ [→ β̂₁]
Measures by how much the salary increases when education increases by one level point (say, one year).

outside slidesUsually it does not make sense: almost nobody at HCL has zero years of education, so β̂₀ is an extrapolation that anchors the line rather than a real salary. Example: if β̂₀ = 2.0 and β̂₁ = 0.5 (₹ lakh), each extra year of education predicts ₹0.5 lakh more salary, and 16 years predicts 2.0 + 0.5 × 16 = ₹10 lakh. Try it in the lab.

Traps

  • The slides print PR(>F) = 0.027 but never conclude: at α = 0.05, reject equal means.

ANOVA in Python

1Write the basic syntax for an ANOVA in Python.

import statsmodels.api as sm; from statsmodels.formula.api import ols; mdl = ols('DV ~ C(IV)', data=dataset).fit(); anova_tab = sm.stats.anova_lm(mdl, typ=1); print(anova_tab). (A sl. 3)

2What does C() do in the formula?

Treats the IV as categorical. (A sl. 4)

3What does the formula DV ~ C(IV) define?

The relationship between the DV and the IV(s). (A sl. 4)

4What form must the data argument take?

A structured format such as a pandas DataFrame, with the formula's variables as columns. (A sl. 5)

5What is mdl, and what does anova_lm do with it?

mdl is the fitted model from ols(...).fit(); anova_lm performs an ANOVA on it. (A sl. 5)

6What does typ=1 mean?

Type 1, sequential sum of squares: each variable is evaluated in the order it appears in the formula. (A sl. 5)

7What is anova_tab, according to deck A2?

Just an object variable that stores the results of running the ANOVA. (A2 sl. 3)

8Give the example data and the C(Method) row of the output.

A: 85, 89, 76; B: 71, 80, 78; C: 93, 95, 88. df 2, sum_sq 369.56, mean_sq 184.78, F 6.958, PR(>F) 0.0273. (A sl. 6–7)

9Interpret PR(>F) = 0.0273 at α = 0.05.

Reject H₀ that all three methods have equal mean scores: at least one method differs. (outside slides)

10How is F built from the table?

F = mean_sq(Method) / mean_sq(Residual) = 184.78 / 26.56 = 6.958; mean_sq = sum_sq / df. (outside slides)

11What two errors would stop the sl. 3 template from running if copied exactly?

The curly quotes around 'DV ~ C(IV)', and (in A1) the merged line "import statsmodels.api as smfrom statsmodels.formula.api import ols".

12Does a significant ANOVA tell you which methods differ?

No, only that not all means are equal. A post-hoc test (e.g. Tukey HSD) finds B vs C here. (outside slides)

MLR Theory 1

13Why is MLR called a top-down approach?

You first hypothesize a relationship between variables, then empirically test it with well-defined statistical procedures. (M1 sl. 2)

14Give the three names for the DV and for the IV.

DV: outcome / response / criterion. IV: explanatory / predictor / regressor. (M1 sl. 3)

15When must you fix the significance level?

Right at the stage of defining the variables, before empirical testing. (M1 sl. 3)

16State the HCL application and its hypothesis.

HCL wants to estimate a good salary package: what determines salary, and how can a competent package be engineered? Hypothesis: salary is determined by educational qualification. (M1 sl. 4)

17Write the bivariate regression model and name each term.

y = β₀ + β₁x + ε. ε: error/disturbance term; β₀: constant/intercept; β₁: slope. (M1 sl. 5)

18What does the error term contain?

Unobserved terms not included in x. (M1 sl. 5)

19Write the salary model and its predicted form.

sal = β₀ + β₁edu + ε; predicted ŝal = β̂₀ + β̂₁edu, with β̂₀, β̂₁ estimated from data. (M1 sl. 6)

20Interpret β̂₀ in the salary model. Does it make sense?

The salary when education is 0. "You need to decide": usually it is an extrapolation, since few employees have zero education. (M1 sl. 7)

21Interpret β̂₁.

By how much salary increases when education increases by one level point (say, one year). (M1 sl. 7)

22What makes a regression "bivariate" rather than "multiple"?

Bivariate: the DV is explained by only one IV. Multiple: two or more IVs. (M1 sl. 5; "multiple" outside slides)
