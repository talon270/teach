Traps to watch

Each one is planted in a slide example or problem. Read these first.

Trade discounts never reach the books

Record the sale at the invoice price. Slide 3: 150 pens at 500 less 10% is booked as 67,500, not 75,000.

The discount window is exact

"1/10, n/60" from 19 Sept ends on 29 Sept. The 30 Sept payment gets no discount.

Returns go back at the price charged

Returned pens come off at 450 each (after trade discount), not the 500 list price.

Ownership, not delivery, decides

FOB shipping point: the buyer owns goods in transit and bears the loss. FOB destination: the seller does.

Freight-in is inventory cost

It is added to net purchases. Not on slidesFreight-out paid by a seller under FOB destination is a selling expense.

Periodic COGS is a plug

Whatever isn't counted at year end is assumed sold. Not on slidesTheft and breakage hide inside COGS.

LCM uses NRV, not selling price

5.10, F169: price 260 is above cost 250, but NRV is 260 − 26 = 234. Write it down.

Consigned goods are still yours

Goods on consignment with a dealer stay in the consignor's inventory (5.12, item 2).

Inventory errors flip next year

This year's ending inventory is next year's beginning. Overstate it by 1,800 and this year's profit is up 1,800; next year's is down 1,800.

LIFO is banned in India

Not on slidesInd AS 2 (like IAS 2) allows FIFO, weighted average and specific identification. Know LIFO for the exam and for US GAAP comparisons.

Slide 2: the four terms

  • Cash and credit sales: earned when economic ownership passes from seller to buyer
  • Returns: goods returned by the buyer
  • Allowances: defective goods kept by the buyer at a reduced price
  • Trade discount: % reduction on the list/catalogue price
  • Cash discount: % reduction on the invoice price for early cash payment on credit sales

Reading "2/10, n/45"

2 / 10 → 2% off if paid within 10 days

n / 45 → otherwise the net (full) amount is due in 45 days

Not on slidesThe seller records the discount taken as Sales discounts, a contra-revenue account, like Sales returns and allowances.

Not on slidesIn the slide 3 quiz, M's books show the same numbers from the other side: Purchases 67,500, Purchase returns & allowances 9,900, Purchase discounts 404.

Slide 5: freight

Freight is chargeable to the "owner" of goods.

Net cost of purchases = Net purchases + Freight-in

Freight-in is part of what it cost to get the goods ready to sell, so it belongs in inventory, then COGS.

FOB shipping point

Ownership passes when goods leave the seller. The buyer incurs shipping costs.

Slide example: sold merchandise of 5,000 FOB shipping point; lost in transit.

→ The buyer's loss. The seller has a sale and a receivable of 5,000; the buyer still owes it and claims on the carrier or insurer.

FOB destination

Ownership passes when goods reach the buyer. The seller incurs shipping costs.

Slide example: merchandise of 2,000 FOB destination; catches fire in transit.

→ The seller's loss. No sale happened. The 2,000 is written off the seller's inventory.

3Cost of goods sold

Slide 6: Cost of Goods Sold (COGS)

Two ways to track inventory

Perpetual: a continuous record of all purchases and sales. COGS is recorded at each sale.

Periodic: a physical inventory is taken. COGS is worked out once, at period end, from the count.

Not on slidesPerpetual tells you what should be on the shelf. A count under perpetual then reveals shrinkage. Periodic can't separate the two.

What we already haveBeginning inventory
+What we purchasedPurchases
=All that we have to offer to customersGoods available for sale
−All that we soldCOGS
=What we are left with towards the endEnding inventory

Slide 6 formulas

COGS = Goods available for sale − Ending inventory

COGS = Beginning inventory + Net cost of purchases − Ending inventory

Not on slidesThen Gross profit = Net sales − COGS, and Net profit = Gross profit − Operating expenses (the 5.12 layout).

5Inventory valuation and three principles

Slide 9: Inventory Valuation

Lower of cost or market (LCM)

Slide 9 ties "market" to net realizable value and calls it a judgment call.

NRV = Expected selling price − Costs to complete and sell

Inventory = lower of Cost and NRV

Not on slidesThe write-down (cost − NRV) is an expense this period, usually inside COGS. Apply it item by item or to groups of similar items (5.10 does both).

More accounting principles (slide 9)

Conservatism: recognize all losses, anticipate no profits. LCM is its clearest example: cut inventory to NRV, but never raise it above cost.

Neutrality: free from upward or downward bias.

Comparability: consistency across reporting periods. Pick a costing method and keep it.

Not on slidesConservatism and neutrality pull against each other. Excess caution is itself a downward bias, and a big write-down this year inflates next year's margin.

Risk and ethics angle Not on slides

Inventory is where profit is easiest to move. Three levers from these slides: the NRV estimate (the "judgment call" on slide 9) decides how big a write-down is; under LIFO, a well-timed year-end purchase changes COGS and tax (5.13 part 3); and cut-off errors around FOB terms and consignments push profit between years (5.12). For the end-sem PLOs: the risk is misstated profit and inventory, the principles are neutrality and comparability, and the corrective action is a consistent costing method, documented NRV estimates, and a cut-off review at the count.

5.13 Bashir Company: FIFO vs LIFO and a year-end purchase (slide 11)

The facts

Sold 50,000 CDs at ₹28. Beginning inventory 10,000 @ ₹18. Purchases: 15,000 @ ₹19; 10,000 @ ₹21; 20,000 @ ₹22; 10,000 @ ₹23. Operating expenses ₹185,000. Income tax 30%.

  1. Net profit under FIFO and LIFO.
  2. Which method is better for (a) income tax and (b) shareholder reporting? Trade-offs, if the same method must be used for both?
  3. Effect of buying 15,000 CDs @ ₹25 on the last day of the period.

65,000 units available − 50,000 sold = 15,000 left (periodic system assumed).

COGS workings

FIFO sells the oldest 50,000: 10,000 @ 18 + 15,000 @ 19 + 10,000 @ 21 + 15,000 @ 22 = 1,005,000. Left: 5,000 @ 22 + 10,000 @ 23 = 340,000.

LIFO sells the newest 50,000: 10,000 @ 23 + 20,000 @ 22 + 10,000 @ 21 + 10,000 @ 19 = 1,070,000. Left: 5,000 @ 19 + 10,000 @ 18 = 275,000.

Part 2: which method?

(a) Tax: LIFO. Prices are rising, so LIFO's COGS is 65,000 higher and tax is 19,500 lower. That is real cash saved.

(b) Shareholders: FIFO shows 45,500 more profit and an inventory figure closer to current cost.

Trade-off: with one method for both, the company chooses between cash (LIFO's tax saving) and reported earnings (FIFO). Profit that only looks higher doesn't pay a dividend; the tax saving does.

What 5.12 is testing Not on slides

Cut-off: who owns goods in transit at year end. FOB terms decide (items 1, 3, 4).

Ownership vs possession: consigned goods are with the dealer but belong to Goodwill (item 2).

Errors that reverse: 20X3's ending inventory is 20X4's beginning, so item 1 moves 600 from one year to the other. Over the two years combined, only items 2 and 5 change total profit.

Errors that cancel: item 3 understates both purchases and ending inventory, so profit is right but the balance sheet isn't.

Goods listed at 20,000, trade discount 15%, terms 2/10, n/30. The buyer pays on day 8. Cash received?

Answer

Invoice 17,000; discount 340; cash 16,660.

Decode "3/15, n/60".

Answer

3% off the invoice if paid within 15 days; otherwise the full amount is due within 60 days.

A buyer keeps scratched goods and the seller knocks 1,000 off the bill. Return or allowance?

Answer

Allowance: the goods are kept at a reduced price.

Purchases 80,000; purchase returns 5,000; purchase discounts 1,500; freight-in 2,000. Net cost of purchases?

Answer

80,000 − 5,000 − 1,500 + 2,000 = 75,500.

Goods shipped FOB shipping point on 30 Dec arrive on 3 Jan. Whose inventory at 31 Dec?

Answer

The buyer's. Ownership passed when the goods left the seller.

Beginning inventory 12,000; net cost of purchases 75,500; ending inventory 9,500. COGS?

Answer

12,000 + 75,500 − 9,500 = 78,000.

Three units bought at 100, 120 and 140; two sold (periodic). COGS under FIFO, LIFO and WAC?

Answer

FIFO 220; LIFO 260; WAC 120 × 2 = 240.

Prices are rising. Which method gives the highest profit, and which the lowest ending inventory?

Answer

Highest profit: FIFO. Lowest ending inventory: LIFO.

An item cost 500. It will sell for 560, with 80 of selling costs. Carrying value?

Answer

NRV 560 − 80 = 480 < 500, so 480. Write down 20.

Name the principle: (a) book the expected loss on obsolete stock now but ignore an expected gain; (b) use FIFO every year.

Answer

(a) Conservatism; (b) comparability.

This year's ending inventory is overstated by 3,000. Effect on this year's and next year's profit?

Answer

This year +3,000 (COGS too low); next year −3,000 (beginning inventory too high). Two-year total: no effect.

Why can a year-end purchase cut profit under LIFO but not under FIFO?

Answer

LIFO charges the newest costs to COGS, so the expensive new units become this year's expense. FIFO leaves them in ending inventory.
