Feminist critiques

Carol Cohn spent a year among US defense intellectuals and learned their language. She found that the language itself makes nuclear war thinkable. Choi and Eschle ask feminists to go further and take coloniality seriously.

Your win todayExplain "technostrategic" language with two examples, and state Choi & Eschle's call.

1 · Warm-up from lesson 10

Ritchie's two nuclear ontologies, and why bridge-building fails.

Hegemonic nuclearism vs subaltern antinuclearism. The contest is agonistic; bridge-building denies that and closes debate.

2 · Cohn (1987): sex and death in the rational world of defense intellectuals

  • Defense analysts speak a specialised "technostrategic" language. It both reflects and shapes the US nuclear project, and lets its speakers think and act as they do.
  • It cleans up violence: "clean bombs" (fusion weapons with less fallout but more blast); "collateral damage" for dead civilians ("The Air Force doesn't target people, it targets shoe factories"); a "surgically clean" counterforce strike that would still kill millions.
  • Learning the language changed what she could think and say: some thoughts became unsayable within it.
  • Her conclusion: technostrategic discourse works less as the basis of decisions than as "an ideological curtain" that legitimises outcomes reached for other reasons. Feminists must choose carefully whether to speak it.

What does Cohn call the language of defense intellectuals?

"Technostrategic" language.

3 · Choi & Eschle (2022): rethinking feminism

  • Feminist and postcolonial IR have mostly talked past each other on nuclear politics.
  • Feminist IR should take fuller account of coloniality, "a colonial matrix of power that is constitutive of modernity" (Mignolo and Walsh), with its racialised underpinnings.
  • That means provincialising western nuclear experience and building location-sensitive, non-hierarchical accounts.
  • Such an approach recasts nuclear destruction as a lived reality for many (testing sites, mining, colonised islands), within a longer history of domination.
Links. Cohn's sanitised language echoes Fraise's "magic" (lesson 6). Choi & Eschle's lived destruction echoes Higuchi and Todeschini (lesson 3) and Hecht's African miners (lesson 4).
