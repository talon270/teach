The bomb's victims

Week 3's four readings share one question: who got to say what radiation does to people? In each, states and scientists held that "definitional power", and the people exposed had to fight it.

Your win todayGive each reading's argument in one line, with one coined term each.

1 · Warm-up from lesson 2

Baruch Plan vs Soviet plan: one line each. What did Pugwash grow out of?

Baruch: supranational authority, no veto, sanctions. Soviet: ban first, inspect later. Pugwash grew out of the fallout controversy (Kraft).

2 · The four readings

ReadingArgumentKey term
Higuchi (2020)1945–53: the US, USSR and Britain treated fallout outside test sites as an "emergency" only (big, quick doses), while secretly tracking it worldwide (Project Gabriel, Project Sunshine). This deepened ignorance of low-dose harm to ranchers, Kazakh villagers, Aboriginal Australians. Their "definitional power" cracked after Castle Bravo (1954).Compartmentalized production of knowledge
Todeschini (1999)US and Japanese science made biomedicine "a hegemonic force" in defining hibakusha (bomb survivors). Women survivors' experience was "repressed and denied": occupation censorship, pollution beliefs, the ABCC's no-treatment policy ("guinea pigs"), relief laws that medicalised suffering.Illegitimate sufferers; "an event without actors"
Lindee (2016)The RERF (the ABCC's successor, 1975) is the "gold standard" of radiation risk, yet distrusted. Dosimetry is "a fundamentally humanistic exercise"; citizenship became a scientific credential; military and civilian science cannot be separated. After Fukushima, survivors turned against nuclear power.Citizenship as credential
Goldstein & Stawkowski (2015)The Hiroshima–Nagasaki data stay "hegemonic" on low-dose genetic effects partly because Lysenkoism stigmatised Soviet genetics. James Neel found no effect in survivors' children; Yuri Dubrova (1996) found doubled mutation rates in Chernobyl children. US and Soviet data "co-configured" today's knowledge.Neel vs Dubrova

Which author calls women survivors "illegitimate sufferers"?

Maya Todeschini (1999), from interviews of 1991–93 and survivors' testimony.
Course lens. Whose order? The nuclear powers'. Which actors? Scientists, states, and the exposed (hibakusha, downwinders, Kazakh villagers, Aboriginal Australians). What counts as "nuclear harm"? Whatever the powerful could define and measure.
