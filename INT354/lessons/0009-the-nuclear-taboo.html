The nuclear taboo

No state has used a nuclear weapon in war since August 1945. The usual explanation is deterrence. Nina Tannenwald says deterrence is "either wrong or incomplete": a norm that stigmatises nuclear use, a taboo, also holds leaders back.

Your win todayGive the anomalies deterrence cannot explain, the three effects of norms, and three signs the taboo may be weakening.

1 · Warm-up from lesson 8

Sagan's three operational requirements for stable deterrence?

No preventive war during the transition; survivable second-strike forces; no accidental or unauthorized use.

2 · Tannenwald (1999): the normative basis of non-use

  • Non-use since 1945 is "the single most important phenomenon of the nuclear age".
  • Anomalies for deterrence: (1) non-use when the enemy could not retaliate: the US monopoly years, Vietnam, the 1991 Gulf War; Britain in the Falklands; the USSR in Afghanistan. (2) Non-nuclear states attacked nuclear ones anyway: China in Korea, North Vietnam, Argentina in the Falklands, Iraq in 1991.
  • Norms have three kinds of effect: regulative (constraining), constitutive (shaping identities and categories such as "weapons of mass destruction"), and permissive (a subset of constitutive: banning one weapon can legitimise others, such as massive conventional bombing).
  • Evidence: "taboo talk", explicit reference to a taboo in decision making, as a cost or as non-cost-benefit reasoning. Her cases are from the US record.
  • The taboo is "not (yet) a fully robust norm".

Which effect of the taboo can make destructive conventional war more acceptable?

The permissive effect, a subset of constitutive effects.

3 · Tannenwald (2018): how strong is it today?

  • A 73-year tradition: Truman and Eisenhower in Korea, no use at Dien Bien Phu (1954), Kennedy and Khrushchev in the Cuban Missile Crisis.
  • Warning signs: US and Russian doctrines that lower the bar (Russia's "escalate to de-escalate" with low-yield weapons); new survey research (Sagan and Valentino) suggesting the American public is not strongly opposed to nuclear use; a US president no one can stop from using them.
  • The 2017 Ban Treaty (TPNW) outlaws use, threat, testing, possession and more, adopted without the nuclear powers.
  • To strengthen the taboo: no-first-use pledges, a smaller role for nuclear weapons, ratifying the CTBT, education on nuclear effects, and engaging with the Ban Treaty.
