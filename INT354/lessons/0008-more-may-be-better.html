INT354 · Lesson 8Week 11 · The Sagan–Waltz debate≈ 25 min

More better or worse?

Lesson 7 asked why states build the bomb. This lesson asks what happens when they do. Kenneth Waltz, a proliferation optimist, says the slow spread of nuclear weapons may make war less likely. Scott Sagan, a pessimist, says real militaries will not behave as the theory needs.

Your win todayArgue each side in three lines, including Sagan's three operational requirements.

1 · Warm-up from lesson 7

Name Sagan's three models of why states build the bomb.

Security, domestic politics, norms.

2 · Waltz: more may be better

  • He says "spread" not "proliferation": weapons have spread slowly. Membership grew, then dropped as South Africa, Kazakhstan, Belarus and Ukraine gave theirs up.
  • States live in self-help under anarchy. Since 1945 there has been the longest peace among major powers in modern history.
  • Deterrence (frightening an attacker with punishment) differs from defense (making an attack hard). Second-strike forces deter.
  • Because the costs of nuclear war are so easy to imagine, leaders "shrink in horror" from starting one (Waltz as quoted by Sagan, p. 49; the line is not on the pages of Waltz's chapter in the course PDF). "The likelihood of war decreases as deterrent and defensive capabilities increase." This holds for small powers too, so the gradual spread "is more to be welcomed than feared".
  • He scorns the "ethnocentric" fear that new nuclear states will be irrational: leaders need only be "sensitive to costs", a phrase Waltz takes from Shai Feldman (p. 13).

3 · Sagan: more will be worse

  • Optimists rest on rational deterrence theory. Sagan uses organization theory: militaries have common biases, inflexible routines and parochial interests.
  • Many new nuclear states have military-run governments or weak civilian control, so those biases shape policy.
  • Stable deterrence has three operational requirements: (1) no preventive war while one side has the bomb and the other is building it; (2) survivable second-strike forces; (3) arsenals not prone to accidental or unauthorized use. Optimists assume new states will meet them; Sagan says that is "a very problematic belief".

Which body of theory does Sagan use against rational deterrence?

Organization theory: bounded rationality, routines, parochial military interests.
