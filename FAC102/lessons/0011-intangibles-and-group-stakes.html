Intangibles and goodwill

Two topics that share one idea: you can only record what you can identify and measure. Home-grown brands fail that test. Goodwill passes it only when you buy another business.

Your win todayPlace a stake on the influence ladder, run the equity method, and compute goodwill.

1 · Warm-up from lesson 10

Define recoverable amount. When is an asset impaired?

The higher of fair value less costs of disposal and value in use. Impaired when the carrying amount exceeds it.

2 · Intangible assets

Non-monetary assets without physical substance. To be recognised they must be identifiable: separable (can be sold or licensed alone) or arising from legal or contractual rights (patent, licence). Cost is amortised, usually straight line.

ItemRecognised?
Home-grown brand, customer list, mastheadNo
Purchased brand or patentYes, at cost
GoodwillOnly when bought in a business combination; never amortised, tested for impairment every year
ResearchNo: expensed
DevelopmentYes, once feasibility, intent, ability, future benefit, resources and reliable measurement are all shown
Risk point for analysts. A pharma or consumer-brand company's most valuable assets may be missing from its balance sheet.

4 · The equity method

Record the investment at cost. Then: + your share of the associate's profit; − dividends received. A dividend from an associate is not income: the profit was already counted; the dividend just turns part of the investment into cash.

Z buys 25% of Q for 125. Q earns a profit of 60. Investment balance before any dividend?

125 + 25% × 60 = 125 + 15 = 140.

Cost 45,000; dividend received 3,000; share of profit 5,100; extra depreciation on the fair-value excess 2,400. Carrying amount?

45,000 − 3,000 + 5,100 − 2,400 = 44,700 (Amar and Lahar).

5 · Subsidiaries, NCI and goodwill

For a subsidiary, add 100% of its assets and liabilities, even if you own 60%. Show the outsiders' part as NCI inside equity.

Goodwill = consideration + NCI − fair value of net assets. Always fair value, never book value.

  • Partial goodwill: NCI = NCI % × fair value of net assets.
  • Full goodwill: NCI = NCI shares × market price.

D buys 75% of M (1,000 shares) at ₹100 a share: consideration 75,000. Market price 90; fair value of net assets 60,000. Partial: NCI 15,000 → goodwill 30,000. Full: NCI 250 × 90 = 22,500 → goodwill 37,500. The 30,000 premium splits into a normal premium 22,500 (750 × 90 − 45,000) and a control premium 7,500 (750 × 10).

Z pays 300 for 60% of Q. Q's net assets are 400. Partial goodwill?

NCI = 40% × 400 = 160. Goodwill = 300 + 160 − 400 = 60.
