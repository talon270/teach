Adjusting entries

Cash in is not always revenue, and cash out is not always an expense. Accrual accounting records revenue when it is earned and expenses when they are incurred, whatever the cash does. At the end of each period, adjusting entries fix the timing.

Your win todaySpot the timing difference in a scenario and write its adjusting entry with the right amount.

1 · Warm-up from lesson 3

Expand DEAD CLIC. Which side increases each group?

Drawings, Expenses, Assets: debit to increase. Capital, Liabilities, Income: credit to increase.

2 · Two principles

  • Revenue recognition: record revenue when it is earned (realised or realisable), not when cash arrives.
  • Matching: record an expense in the period it is incurred, beside the revenue it helped earn.
Ethics link (end-sem PLOs). Every timing choice is a judgment call. Booking revenue too early, or under-accruing expenses, inflates this period's profit. Risk: misstated profit. Fix: apply recognition and matching consistently and disclose the policy.

3 · The four timing differences

Accrual = the event comes first. Deferral = the cash comes first. If they owe you, it is an asset; if you owe them, a liability.

4 · The adjusting entries

Each one moves one balance-sheet account and one P&L account, and never touches Cash. Method: (1) cross out Cash; (2) name the P&L account and the balance-sheet account; (3) apply DEAD CLIC.

Supplies bought 30,000; supplies on hand at month end 23,000. What is the supplies expense?

Used = available − on hand = 30,000 − 23,000 = 7,000. Dr Supplies expense, Cr Supplies.

Year ends 31 July. A ₹15,000 bill at 14% was issued on 1 January. How much interest must you accrue?

January to July = 7 months. 15,000 × 14% × 7 ÷ 12 = 1,225. Dr Interest expense, Cr Interest payable.

Year ends 31 July. A 2-year fire policy costing ₹12,000 started on 1 September. Insurance expense for this year?

12,000 ÷ 24 = 500 a month. September to July = 11 months. 11 × 500 = 5,500.

5 · From cash to accrual

Revenue = cash received + rise in receivables (or unbilled) + fall in unearned revenue. Expense = cash paid + rise in payables + fall in prepaid. Example: fees received in cash 21,710; unbilled revenue up 300; unearned revenue down 500 → fee revenue 22,510.

6 · Closing the books

After adjustments: adjusted trial balance → statements → closing. Temporary accounts (revenue, expenses, dividends) are emptied into retained earnings. Permanent accounts (assets, liabilities, equity) carry forward. So the post-closing trial balance holds balance-sheet accounts only.
