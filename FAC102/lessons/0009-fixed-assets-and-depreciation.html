Fixed assets and depreciation

A machine helps earn revenue for years, so its cost is not one year's expense. You record it at cost, then spread that cost over its useful life as depreciation (matching again).

Your win todayBuild an asset's cost from a list of items, and compute depreciation by four methods, including a part year.

1 · Warm-up from lesson 8

Write the cash conversion cycle formula. Which turnover uses COGS?

CCC = DIO + DSO − DPO. Inventory turnover uses COGS (inventory is at cost).

2 · What counts as a fixed asset

Non-current assets are held for use, not for sale: property, plant and equipment (PPE), intangibles (patents, brands), natural resources. Purpose decides: a truck is inventory for Mahindra (it sells trucks) but a fixed asset for a courier firm.

3 · Cost of acquisition

Cost = net purchase price (after discounts, plus non-recoverable taxes) + directly attributable costs to bring it to the place and condition for use: freight, installation, transit insurance, trial runs. Abnormal or avoidable costs are expensed: breakdown repairs, injury compensation, damage in transit.

Ethics link. Capitalising raises this year's profit; expensing lowers it. Capitalising running costs to flatter profit is a classic manipulation.

Basket purchase: split one price by fair values. ₹900,000 for land (FV 400,000) and building (FV 800,000) → land 300,000, building 600,000. Land is not depreciated.

4 · Depreciation methods

Two estimates: residual value (RV: what you get at disposal, less disposal costs) and useful life (often shorter than physical life). Depreciable amount = cost − RV. Entry: Dr Depreciation expense, Cr Accumulated depreciation. Carrying amount = cost − accumulated depreciation.

All methods charge the same 90,000 in total. Only the timing differs. Accelerated methods (WDV, SYD) charge more early: more conservative.

Cost 50,000; RV 5,000; life 5 years. SYD depreciation in year 2?

Digits 1+2+3+4+5 = 15. Year 2 uses 4 remaining years: 45,000 × 4/15 = 12,000.

Machine bought 1 October for 100,000; RV 10,000; 4-year life; SLM; year ends 31 March. First-year depreciation?

22,500 a year × 6/12 (October to March) = 11,250. Always pro-rate the first year.

5 · Revised estimates go forward only

If the life or RV changes, do not reopen past years. Take the carrying amount now (plus any capitalised major repair), less the new RV, and spread it over the remaining life. A repair that extends life is capital spending; routine maintenance is an expense.
