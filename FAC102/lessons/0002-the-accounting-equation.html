The accounting equation

Every number in accounting hangs on one line: Assets = Liabilities + Equity. What the business owns equals the claims on it: outsiders' claims (liabilities) plus the owners' claim (equity). Every transaction keeps this balanced.

Your win todayPut any transaction into the equation, and find profit from opening and closing equity.

1 · Warm-up from lesson 1

Name the four accounting principles, with a "broken when" example for one of them.

Reporting entity, going concern, periodicity, money measurement. Example: an award booked as income breaks money measurement.

2 · The three elements

ElementPlain wordsExamples
AssetsWhat it owns: resources it controls that give future benefitsCash, inventory, buildings, receivables
LiabilitiesWhat it owes outsiders, from past eventsBank loan, salaries payable, trade payables
EquityWhat is left for the owners: A − LShare capital + revenue − expenses − dividends

So the long form is: A = L + Share capital + Revenues − Expenses − Dividends. Revenue raises equity; expenses and dividends lower it.

Assets 250,000; equity 110,000. What are the liabilities?

L = A − E = 250,000 − 110,000 = 140,000.

Equity is 60,000 and assets are three times liabilities. What are the assets?

3L − L = 2L = 60,000, so L = 30,000 and A = 90,000. Shortcut: if A = k × L, then L = E ÷ (k − 1).

3 · Analysing transactions

Every transaction changes at least two things, and the equation still balances afterwards.

TransactionAssetsLiabilitiesEquity
Owner invests ₹50,000Cash +50,000Capital +50,000
Truck ₹100,000 on creditTruck +100,000Payable +100,000
Supplies ₹18,000 for cashSupplies +18,000; Cash −18,000
Services ₹15,000 for cashCash +15,000Revenue +15,000
Pay the truck supplier ₹16,000Cash −16,000Payable −16,000
Salaries ₹10,000 earned, ₹7,000 paidCash −7,000Salary payable +3,000Expense −10,000
Owner takes ₹1,500 "out of profits"Cash −1,500Dividend −1,500
Five traps. (1) A promise or agreement is not a transaction: no entry. (2) The owner's private spending is not the business's. (3) Paying a supplier is not an expense. (4) Collecting a receivable is not revenue: it was counted when earned. (5) An expense counts when incurred, not when paid.

A client signs a contract for ₹12,000 of work next month. What is the effect on the equation today?

No effect. A promise for future work is not a transaction until something is delivered.

4 · Profit from the change in equity

Equity moves for only three reasons: owners put money in, owners take money out, or the business makes a profit or loss.

Net profit = (Closing equity − Opening equity) − Capital introduced + Dividends

Example: equity rises from 222,500 to 277,000 (up 54,500). The owner added capital of 65,200 and took no dividends. Profit = 54,500 − 65,200 = loss of 10,700.

Opening equity 80,000; closing 105,000; capital added 20,000; dividends 12,000. What is the profit?

25,000 − 20,000 + 12,000 = 17,000. Subtract new capital (not earned); add back dividends (earned, then paid out).

6 · Do it for real (10 min)

On paper, draw columns Cash · Receivables · Supplies · Equipment | Loan | Equity. Start: Cash 4,100; Receivables 3,400; Supplies 1,100; Equipment 10,000; Loan 1,900; Equity 16,700. Post: services on credit 6,100; bank loan 6,000; collect 2,800 of old bills; use supplies 300; interest paid 100. Check: cash 12,800 + receivables 6,700 + supplies 800 + equipment 10,000 = 30,300 = loan 7,900 + equity 22,400.
