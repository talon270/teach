Reading the ratios

An annual report runs to 300 or 500 pages (sl. 47). Analysis shrinks it into a few numbers that say whether a firm earns well, can pay its bills, and is worth its price.

Your win todayCompute and read the deck's ratios, and split ROE into its three DuPont parts.

1 · Warm-up from lesson 14

Indirect method: inventories rose by 1,400 and trade payables fell by 6,700. What do the two do to CFO?

Both reduce it: −1,400 (cash went into stock) and −6,700 (cash went to suppliers). Together −8,100.

2 · The desire to grow

Sl. 46 gives two firms, 2019 and 2024:

A 2019A 2024B 2019B 2024
Revenue30,00080,00030,00060,000
COGS18,00048,00018,00035,400
SGA expenses3,0009,6003,0006,000
Depreciation6,00016,0006,00012,000

The deck asks: which would you invest in, and why? What do their growth paths say?

A's operating profit (EBIT) in 2024 = revenue − COGS − SGA − depreciation?

80,000 − 48,000 − 9,600 − 16,000 = 6,400.

B's EBIT margin in 2024, in %? (B's EBIT = 6,600.)

6,600 / 60,000 = 11%.

Both started at a 10% EBIT margin. A grew revenue 167% but its margin fell to 8%: SGA rose 3.2 times while revenue rose 2.7 times. B grew 100%, kept costs in step, and lifted its margin to 11%. Bigger is not the same as better: growth must be read with margins. (These margins are worked here; the deck leaves the answer open.)

3 · Where the numbers come from, and four ways to read them

Sources beyond the annual report (sl. 49): stock exchanges, business websites and media, credit rating agencies, databases, the government and RBI, social media. Disclosure is driven by regulation, capital, product and managerial labour markets, and litigation, and it lowers the cost of capital (sl. 48).

Fundamental analysis (sl. 50)What it does
HorizontalYear on year change
TrendSeveral years, each as % of a base year
VerticalOne year, each item as % of a base item: common-size statements
RatioRelations between items

Qualitative data also count: ownership, the board, EVA, CSR, analyst calls.

4 · The profit ladder

Sl. 52: Revenue − COGS = gross profit. − operating expenses (SGA, R&D, depreciation and amortisation) = operating profit (EBIT); before depreciation and amortisation it is EBITDA. − interest = PBT. − taxes = PAT.

Firm A, 2024: EBITDA?

80,000 − 48,000 − 9,600 = 22,400. Subtract depreciation 16,000 to reach EBIT 6,400.

"Profit" can be PAT, PBT, EBIT, EBITDA or NOPAT (sl. 53): say which one you use. Quick assets are current assets less inventories (and prepaid expenses).

DuPont (sl. 56): ROE = ROA × leverage = (Profit / Sales) × (Sales / Assets) × (Assets / Equity).

Practice firm (numbers made up for this lesson): sales 50,000; PAT 4,000; total assets 40,000; equity 16,000; debt 15,000; total liabilities 24,000; current assets 12,000 of which inventories 4,000; current liabilities 8,000; EBIT 7,000; interest 1,500; 1,000 shares; price ₹60; dividend ₹1.20 a share.

ROE in %?

4,000 / 16,000 = 25%. DuPont: margin 8% × turnover 1.25 × leverage 2.5 = 25%.

Quick ratio?

(12,000 − 4,000) / 8,000 = 1.0. The current ratio is 12,000 / 8,000 = 1.5.

Interest coverage (two decimals)?

7,000 / 1,500 = 4.67.

P/E ratio?

EPS = 4,000 / 1,000 = 4. P/E = 60 / 4 = 15. Dividend yield = 1.20 / 60 = 2%; book value per share = 16,000 / 1,000 = 16, so P/B = 60 / 16 = 3.75.
