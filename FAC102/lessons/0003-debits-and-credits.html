Debits and credits

Lesson 2 tracked transactions as + and − in the equation. Real books use debit (Dr, left side) and credit (Cr, right side) instead. "Debit" does not mean "money out". It only means the left side of an account.

Your win todayWrite the journal entry for any common transaction, and say what a trial balance can and cannot catch.

1 · Warm-up from lesson 2

A customer pays ₹4,000 that they owed. What happens to assets, liabilities, equity and revenue?

Cash +4,000, receivables −4,000. Liabilities, equity and revenue do not change.

2 · DEAD CLIC

Move expenses and dividends to the left of the equation: Assets + Expenses + Dividends = Liabilities + Capital + Income. Everything on the left grows with a debit. Everything on the right grows with a credit.

Debit to increase (DEA D)Credit to increase (CLIC)
Drawings / dividendsCapital (share capital)
Expenses: rent, salaries, depreciationLiabilities: payables, loans, unearned revenue
Assets: cash, receivables, prepaid rent, landIncome: sales, service revenue
Contra-revenue: sales returns, sales discountsContra-asset: accumulated depreciation, provision for bad debts

A contra account sits beside its partner and reduces it, so it takes the opposite side.

Unearned revenue increases. Debit or credit?

Credit. Unearned revenue is a liability: you owe the customer work.

Prepaid rent increases. Debit or credit?

Debit. Prepaid rent is an asset: a future benefit you already paid for.

3 · The journal entry

Each transaction goes first into the journal: debit line first, credit line indented below, then a short narration.

2 June   Dr  Office supplies        15,000
              Cr  Trade payables          15,000
         (office supplies bought on credit)

Then each line is posted to its ledger account (a T account per item). Debits always equal credits in every entry.

TransactionDebitCredit
Owner invests cashCashShare capital
Pay this month's rentRent expenseCash
Pay a year's rent in advancePrepaid rentCash
Services on creditAccounts receivableService revenue
Customer pays in advanceCashUnearned revenue
Supplies 15,000; 9,000 paid nowSupplies 15,000Cash 9,000; Payables 6,000
Owner withdraws cashDrawingsCash
Land rises in valueNo entry: land stays at cost
Bank statements are back to front. To the bank, your balance is a liability, so your deposit shows as a credit on its statement. In your own books, cash is an asset: a deposit is a debit.

4 · The trial balance

A trial balance lists every ledger balance on one date and checks that total debits = total credits. It only proves the arithmetic. These errors still balance:

  • A transaction left out entirely.
  • The right amount posted to the wrong account, on the correct side.
  • An entry with debit and credit swapped.
  • Equal errors on both sides.

5 · The four statements

StatementShows
Statement of Profit and Loss (income statement)Revenue − expenses = profit, for a period
Balance Sheet (statement of financial position)A = L + E, on a date
Statement of Changes in EquityOpening equity + profit − dividends ± share issues = closing equity
Statement of Cash FlowsCash from operating, investing and financing activities

They link: profit flows into retained earnings, retained earnings sits in the balance sheet's equity, and dividends appear both as a fall in retained earnings and as a financing cash outflow. Depreciation cuts profit but uses no cash.

7 · Do it for real (10 min)

Journalise on paper, then build a trial balance: owner invests 100,000 cash; supplies 15,000 (9,000 cash, rest on credit); land 48,000 cash; owner withdraws 2,000; supplies 4,000 cash. Check: Cash 37,000, Land 48,000, Supplies 19,000, Drawings 2,000 = 106,000 Dr; Payables 6,000 + Capital 100,000 = 106,000 Cr.
