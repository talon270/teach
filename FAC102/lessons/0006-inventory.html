Inventory methods

When identical units were bought at different prices, which cost goes to COGS and which stays on the shelf? The answer changes profit, tax and the balance sheet. This is the most numerical lesson so far: keep a pen ready.

Your win todayCompute ending inventory and COGS under FIFO, LIFO and weighted average, value stock at the lower of cost and NRV, and trace an inventory error.

1 · Warm-up from lesson 5

Write the COGS formula and the goods-available formula.

Goods available = opening inventory + net cost of purchases. COGS = goods available − closing inventory.

2 · The methods

MethodUnits sold are the…Prices rising: effect
FIFO (first in, first out)Oldest boughtLowest COGS, highest profit, stock near current cost
LIFO (last in, first out)Newest boughtHighest COGS, lowest profit and tax
Weighted average (WAC)Average: total cost ÷ total unitsIn between
Specific identificationThe actual unitsFor unique items: cars, jewellery

Ind AS 2 does not allow LIFO in India, but the exam still compares it. Under a perpetual system the average is recomputed after each purchase: the moving average. FIFO gives the same answer under both systems.

3 · One month, worked

Opening 100 @ 10. Bought 200 @ 12, then 100 @ 14, then 100 @ 15. Sold 350 units in total. Available: 500 units costing 6,300. Left: 150 units.

Ending inventory (150)100 @ 15 + 50 @ 14 = 2,200100 @ 10 + 50 @ 12 = 1,600150 × 12.60 = 1,890

Method: find ending inventory first, then COGS = available − ending. WAC = 6,300 ÷ 500 = 12.60 (total cost ÷ total units, not an average of the prices).

Opening 50 @ 8; bought 100 @ 10, then 50 @ 12. 120 units sold. FIFO ending inventory?

200 units available, 80 left. FIFO keeps the newest: 50 @ 12 = 600 + 30 @ 10 = 300. Ending inventory = 900.

Same data. Weighted-average (periodic) ending inventory?

Total cost 400 + 1,000 + 600 = 2,000 for 200 units = 10.00 each. 80 × 10 = 800.

4 · Lower of cost and NRV

NRV (net realisable value) = expected selling price − costs to complete and sell. Inventory is shown at the lower of cost and NRV. The write-down is an expense now. Inventory is never written up above cost. Apply it item by item (more cautious) or by groups of similar items.

Cost 400 a unit; selling price 450; costs to sell 70. Value per unit?

NRV = 450 − 70 = 380, below cost 400. Value at 380 and write down 20 a unit.

This is conservatism: recognise all losses, anticipate no profits. It pulls against neutrality (no bias either way). Keep one costing method across periods: comparability.

Ethics link. Inventory is where profit is easiest to move: the NRV estimate, a year-end purchase under LIFO (it flows straight into COGS), and cut-off errors around FOB terms. Corrective action: a consistent method, documented NRV estimates, and a cut-off review at the count.

5 · Inventory errors flip

Ending inventory overstated → COGS understated → profit overstated this year. That figure becomes next year's opening inventory → COGS overstated → profit understated next year. Over two years the error cancels.

Cut-off rules: goods sent out on consignment are still yours. Goods bought FOB shipping point are yours once shipped, even if still on the truck.
