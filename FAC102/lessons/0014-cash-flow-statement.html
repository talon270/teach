Cash flows

Profit is an opinion; cash is a fact. The cash flow statement shows where cash came from and where it went, in three boxes. You will build the operating box for one company two ways and get the same number.

Your win todaySort any cash flow into its box, and compute cash from operations (CFO) by the direct and the indirect method.

1 · Warm-up from lesson 9

A firm records ₹6,700 of depreciation this year. How much cash leaves the firm because of it?

None. Depreciation spreads an old purchase over the years; the cash left when the asset was bought. So depreciation is a non-cash expense, and it comes back in today's indirect method.

2 · Why a cash flow statement

The deck (sl. 30) gives the reasons: more frauds, more failures and less credibility for reported profit put more weight on cash. Cash flows help to judge liquidity, flexibility and risk, and the statement is now mandated (in India, Ind AS 7).

The deck's quick quiz (sl. 32): paid suppliers → operating; received interest on a loan → investing; sold a building → investing; issued shares → financing; repaid a loan → financing; collected old dues → operating. Exchanged a desktop for a laptop → no cash, so not in the statement.

4 · Direct method: count the cash

CFO = cash from customers − cash to suppliers, providers and employees − income tax paid (sl. 34). Sl. 37 turns each line into accounts you can read:

  1. Cash from customers = Sales + decrease (− increase) in trade receivables − bad debts written off.
  2. Cash to suppliers and staff = COGS + increase (− decrease) in inventories + decrease (− increase) in trade payables + SGA* + increase (− decrease) in prepaid expenses. *SGA without non-cash items such as bad debts.
  3. Income tax paid = tax expense + decrease (− increase) in tax payable.

Customers. The formula subtracts write-offs, so use gross receivables (net + allowance): 8,200 against 6,100, an increase of 2,100. Cash = 75,800 − 2,100 − 700 = 73,000.

Suppliers and staff. 43,900 + 1,400 (inventory up) + 6,700 (payables down) + 7,000 (SGA 8,500 − bad debts 1,500) + 300 (prepaid up) = 59,300.

Income tax paid?

8,300 + 700 (tax payable fell from 2,800 to 2,100, so the firm paid more than the expense) = 9,000.

CFO by the direct method?

73,000 − 59,300 − 9,000 = 4,700. Interest paid is financing and interest or dividends received are investing (sl. 31), so none of them is here.

5 · Indirect method: start from profit

Sl. 38: take net profit, then adjust for non-cash items (depreciation, bad debts), non-operating items (interest, gains and losses on sales) and working capital (receivables, payables, unearned revenue).

Sign rule. A current asset that rises took cash: subtract it. A current liability that rises kept cash in: add it. Losses and expenses that used no cash: add back. Gains and income that belong to investing or financing: take out.

8,900 + 6,700 + 800 − 1,200 − 900 − 300 + 1,100 = 15,100.

6 · Investing, financing, free cash flow

Investing (sl. 41): buying and selling tangible and intangible assets and investments, insurance settlements, interest and dividends received. Financing (sl. 42): raising and repaying capital and loans, interest and dividends paid.

Free cash flow = CFO − cash flow from investing (sl. 41): the cash left after the firm pays for the assets it needs. Read "cash flow from investing" as the cash spent there.

Dutt Company: cash flow margin, in % (one decimal)?

4,700 / 75,800 = 6.2%. Its profit margin is 8,900 / 75,800 = 11.7%, so cash lags profit: the payables fell by 6,700.
