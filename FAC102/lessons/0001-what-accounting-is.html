What accounting is

Accounting is the language of business: it measures, records, summarises and reports what a business does. Before any numbers, you need the vocabulary: what kind of business, owned how, reported to whom, and under which rules.

Your win todayClassify a business and a company form, and name the principle or agency problem behind a short scenario.

1 · Three types of business

Ask one question: what does it do with the product?

TypeTestExamples
ManufacturingMakes the product itselfTata Steel, Amul, Haldiram
Trading (merchandising)Buys finished goods and resells themD-Mart, Croma, Westside
ServiceSells an activity, not a goodInfosys, IndiGo, Zomato
Trap. A shop that sells other firms' brands is trading, not manufacturing.

A listed public company's shares trade on a stock exchange, so it faces the most scrutiny and has independent directors. An unlisted public company's shares do not.

What is the maximum number of partners in a partnership?

100. Private company: 200. Public company: at least 7, no maximum.

3 · Who reads the accounts

Shareholders, lenders, employees, customers, suppliers, government, unions, competitors, analysts and the public. A business has three goals: earn profits, survive (liquidity: pay bills when due) and maximise value. A firm can show a profit and still fail to pay salaries: then survival is at risk, not profit.

4 · Agency problems and ethics

Shareholders (the principals) hire managers (the agents). The managers know more than the owners: information asymmetry. That gap causes the agency problems.

ProblemBetweenSpot it
Type 1 (principal–agent)Owners vs managersEmpire building, short-term targets
Adverse selectionOwners vs a candidateBefore the deal: hidden information
Moral hazardOwners vs the hired CEOAfter the deal: hidden actions
Type 2 (principal–principal)Majority vs minority ownersBig owners override small ones

Fixes: signalling, disclosure, and disciplining managers (governance, monitoring, audit, regulation). The external auditor reports to the shareholders; the internal auditor and the audit committee report to the board. This is the ethics and compliance material the end-sem tests (PLO 6.1, 6.2).

5 · Standards and the four principles

IFRS (set by the IASB) is principle-based. US GAAP (set by the FASB) is rule-based. India's Ind AS follow IFRS.

PrincipleMeansBroken when
Reporting entityThe business is separate from its ownersThe promoter's wedding is booked as a company expense
Going concernAssume the business keeps operatingThe company is heading for insolvency
PeriodicityReport for regular periodsA year's results are reported a year late
Money measurementRecord only what money can measure₹10 crore booked for a CEO award

Two quick tests: "Whose is it?" → reporting entity. "Can it be priced?" → money measurement.
