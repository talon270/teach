Receivables and bad debts

Selling on credit wins sales, but some customers never pay. You don't know which ones yet, so you estimate the loss in the same year as the sale (matching) and park it in a provision.

Your win todayCompute the bad-debt expense under both methods, and write the write-off and recovery entries.

1 · Warm-up from lesson 6

Define NRV for inventory, and say what you do when NRV is below cost.

NRV = selling price − costs to complete and sell. If NRV < cost, write the inventory down to NRV and expense the difference.

2 · Cash and cash equivalents

Cash = money available on demand: notes, coins, cheques, bank deposits. A post-dated cheque is not cash: it cannot be banked yet, so it is a receivable. Cash equivalents are short-term, highly liquid investments with an insignificant risk of change in value, usually maturing within 3 months of purchase: Treasury bills, commercial paper. Shares are never cash equivalents.

3 · The provision entry

Dr  Bad debt expense                   X
     Cr  Provision for doubtful debts       X

The provision is a contra-asset (credit balance). Balance sheet: trade receivables − provision = NRV of receivables. Other name: allowance for credit losses.

4 · Two methods, two expense rules

MethodEstimatesBad debt expense =
% of credit sales (income-statement approach)This year's expenseThe calculated amount. Ignore the old balance.
% of receivables or ageing (balance-sheet approach)The closing provision balanceRequired − existing credit balance (or + an existing debit balance)

Ageing applies a higher % to older debts. Example: not yet due 800 @ 2% = 16; 1–30 days 400 @ 4% = 16; 31–60 days 200 @ 6% = 12; over 60 days 100 @ 10% = 10. Required provision = 54.

Ageing says the provision must be 54. The provision already has a credit balance of 20. Bad debt expense?

Balance-sheet method: top up the difference. 54 − 20 = 34.

Required provision 54, but write-offs left the provision with a debit balance of 6. Expense?

54 + 6 = 60. A debit balance must first be cleared, then the provision built to 54.

Net credit sales 200,000; the firm uses 2% of credit sales. The provision already holds 1,500. Expense?

% of sales gives the expense directly: 200,000 × 2% = 4,000. The old balance does not matter.

5 · Write-off and recovery

EventDebitCredit
Write off a known bad debtProvisionTrade receivables
Recovery, step 1: reverse the write-offTrade receivablesProvision
Recovery, step 2: record the cashCashTrade receivables

A write-off changes neither profit nor NRV: the expense was booked when the provision was made, and receivables and provision fall together. The direct write-off method (expense only when a debt goes bad) breaks matching, so the course uses the provision method.
