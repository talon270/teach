Synthesis for the end-sem

The end-sem rewards synthesis across weeks and approaches. A strong answer names the author, the approach, one case or instrument, and one counter-voice, and runs one question across three or more weeks.

Your win todayOutline one essay from memory using four readings, then pass a mixed quiz.

1 · The three course questions

  1. What is IL, and who makes it? Sources (Shaw); those who designate sources (Charlesworth); decisions (Ratner); a norm hierarchy (Kelsen); the sovereign decision (Schmitt); stories lawyers tell (d'Aspremont).
  2. How does power shape it? Empire (Anghie, Chimni); capital (Chimni, Knox); race (Mbori, Kourtis, Girijashanker); caste (Vishwanath); gender (Charlesworth); great powers (Hathaway & Shapiro, Schmitt); the Court's discretion (Bedjaoui).
  3. Which approach? Doctrinal, positivist, realist, natural law, decisionist, feminist, TWAIL, Marxist, critical race, critical history, Dalit lens.

2 · Approach map: match from memory

Give the approach of each: Anghie · Kelsen · Ratner · Schmitt · Knox · Charlesworth · d'Aspremont · Vishwanath.

Anghie: TWAIL. Kelsen: positivism (pure theory). Ratner: realism. Schmitt: decisionism. Knox: Marxist (and TWAIL). Charlesworth: feminist. d'Aspremont: critical history. Vishwanath: Dalit lens.

3 · Three essay frames

QuestionSkeleton
"IL is a European creation universalised." Discuss.Standard story (Shaw; Art 38's "civilised nations") → born in empire (Anghie), capital (Chimni), racial grammar (Mbori) → TWAIL's "truly universal" hope (Anand, Elias, Bedjaoui) → d'Aspremont: still European markers → universality contested by race and caste (Girijashanker, Vishwanath).
"Who makes international law?"States by consent (Shaw, Lotus) → those who designate sources (Charlesworth 2012) → judges with expediency (Bedjaoui), decision-makers (Ratner) → the norm hierarchy (Kelsen) vs the sovereign of the exception (Schmitt) → missing makers: women (Charlesworth 1999), Dalits (Vishwanath).
"Race and caste are central, not marginal, to IL."The Indian as non-sovereign other (Anghie) → sovereign equality, mandates, veto (Mbori) → law constructs race (Kourtis) → India's interest convergence and double-move (Girijashanker) → caste and the liberal subject (Vishwanath).

Without looking: write a five-line skeleton for "Does outlawing war work?" using four readings.

Kellogg–Briand and Art 2(4) outlawed conquest; outcasting kept peace (Hathaway & Shapiro) → Schmitt: criminalising war makes it total → Knox: enforcement is selective; hypocrisy is structural (Crimea, Ukraine) → Kourtis: law primed conditions for genocide in Gaza → close on who enforces now (H&S: middle powers, the GA).
