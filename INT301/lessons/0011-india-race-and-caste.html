India, race and caste

India fought South African racism at the UN. It also resists any international scrutiny of caste. Girijashanker explains the pattern; Vishwanath asks why human-rights law cannot see Dalits as producers of legal knowledge.

Your win todayExplain interest convergence, the double-move, and the Three Rs, each with one fact.

1 · Warm-up from lesson 10

What is turntablism, in one line?

New historiographical "sounds" made from the same records: same markers, periodisation and causal sequencing (d'Aspremont).

2 · Girijashanker: Indian approaches to racism

  • Derrick Bell's interest convergence: racial justice, "or its appearance", advances when it serves dominant interests.
  • India led the UN fight: Res 44 (I), 1946 on Indians in South Africa (trigger: the "Ghetto Act"; hurdle: Art 2(7) domestic jurisdiction; diluted by Mexico and France) to Res 1761, 1962 (condemned apartheid; sanctions; a Special Committee). But it did so for geopolitical interest, while keeping hierarchies (Bajpai's "backward continent"; B.N. Rau's "casteist solution").
  • Under scrutiny India makes a double-move: invoke its antiracist history and deny that the instrument applies. Caste is not "descent" under ICERD Art 1(1) (India's position from 1996), against CERD General Recommendation XXIX (2002), which says descent covers caste. At Durban 2001, India kept caste out, arguing non-interference (Omar Abdullah), which was South Africa's own 1946 argument.
  • Proposal: a cross-bloc coalition (OIC, African states, Nigeria); reputation as the lever.

What is Derrick Bell's theory that racial justice advances when it serves dominant interests?

Interest convergence.

3 · Vishwanath: bodies of knowledge

  • IL treats Dalits as passive recipients, not active producers of legal discourse, erasing an epistemology produced from their bodies: "where is the body in international law?"
  • Caste law in India: Art 17 abolishes untouchability; the SC/ST (Prevention of Atrocities) Act 1989. Yet 57,582 cases (2018–22), and Khuman Singh (2019), where the Supreme Court read "only" into s 3(2)(v), diluting it.
  • Dalit praxis, the Three Rs: resilience (domestic law), resourcefulness (IL; the Dalit Radical Tradition), resistance (writing, poetry).
  • IHRL's liberal subject fails Dalits (Mutua's savages–victims–saviours; Kapur). IHRL must become non-liberal, non-Western and non-Brahmanical.
Quiz trap. Girijashanker explains why India deflects (interest, reputation). Vishwanath explains why IHRL cannot see Dalits (the liberal subject). CERD 1996 / GR XXIX 2002 = the treaty body says descent includes caste. Durban 2001 = the world conference where India kept caste out.
