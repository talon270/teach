Natural law vs positivism

Is law binding because it is right, or because it was made in the proper way? That is the oldest split in legal theory, and the tutorials' core contrast. Every theorist in this course sits somewhere on it.

Your win todayState the tenets of legal positivism, place Vitoria, Grotius, Kelsen and Hart, and explain the Lotus presumption.

3 · Kammerhofer: what positivism means

"Legal positivism" is so unclear it is "positively unhelpful", so Kammerhofer lists typical tenets: law identified by sources (form, not content); positive law made by human will; separation of law and morals; rejection of natural law; relative values; the gap between "is" and "ought".

Classical IL positivism adds: state consent; states as the original subjects; restrictive interpretation; and the Lotus presumption (PCIJ, 1927): what is not prohibited is permitted; restrictions on states are not presumed.

As practice, positivism is a "culture of orthodoxy". His verdict: "problematic and utterly muddled", but "ubiquitous and vital".
