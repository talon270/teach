The World Court

Mohammed Bedjaoui sat on the ICJ and was its president. His two articles show the Court from inside: first how a judgment is built (1991), then why judges must use judgment beyond strict law (2001).

Your win todayDescribe the ICJ's deliberation steps, and name the five functions of expediency with one case each.

1 · Warm-up from lesson 7

What is outcasting, and why do Hathaway & Shapiro think it is now failing?

Enforcement by excluding violators from cooperation (sanctions), not war. It rested on the expectation that violators pay; Trump's threats destroy that expectation.

2 · Bedjaoui 1991: the manufacture of judgments

Four verbs: read, listen, deliberate, decide.

  • Read: cases usually start by special agreement between the states, since the optional clause disappointed. Written pleadings swell with annexes: Barcelona Traction's weighed 25 kg.
  • Listen: oral hearings before a silent bench, argued by a narrow circle of elite counsel.
  • Deliberate: each judge writes an anonymous written note. Judges speak in inverse order of seniority (most junior first). A Drafting Committee from the majority writes the text; two readings follow.
  • Decide: an oral vote, with no abstention.

A judgment is "merely the highest common denominator of the Judges' views". Separate and dissenting opinions belong to it.

In what order do ICJ judges speak in deliberation?

Inverse order of seniority: the most junior judge speaks first.

3 · Bedjaoui 2001: expediency

Art 38 seems to leave judges only to apply law. But expediency (opportunité) is "the faithful but often discreet companion of applied international law". Legal security is essential but "not enough": the Court must "extinguish a dispute, not fan the flames". Expediency lives inside legality (wisdom, prudence), never opportunism. It goes with judging "as inseparably as a man and his shadow".
