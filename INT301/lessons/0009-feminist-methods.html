Feminist methods

Charlesworth (1999) asks what IL looks like when you make gender a category of analysis. Her answer: rules that sound neutral are built on men's lives, and the gaps (the silences) are where to look.

Your win todayName the two feminist methods and the four layers of the "archaeological dig", with one example.

1 · Warm-up from lesson 8

Name Bedjaoui's four verbs (1991) and two of expediency's five functions (2001).

Read, listen, deliberate, decide. Functions: ruling when the norm fails; declining to rule; individualising law; giving content to vague norms; supplementing a clear norm.

2 · The argument

  • Feminism is not one of "seven rival methodological traditions". Feminist methods favour "conversations and dialogue" and question IL's claim to objectivity.
  • An archaeological dig, layer by layer: (1) women are absent from institutions → (2) the vocabulary → (3) neutral rules with gendered effects → (4) gendered core concepts (states, security, order).
  • Searching for silences: look at the binaries IL relies on: objective/subjective, public/private, protector/protected. Example: the Torture Convention requires a public official, so private violence against women drops out.
  • World traveling: attend to differences among women; no single "woman" speaks for all.
  • Even new international criminal law "remains primarily a system based on men's lives". Rape is framed as an attack on honour or community (Akayesu, ICTR 1998, included). Rape should be a crime against women.

Which method looks at what IL leaves unsaid, such as the public/private split?

Searching for silences. The other method is world traveling.
Note. The outline lists Charlesworth, Chinkin & Wright, "Feminist Approaches to IL" (1991). Your notes use the 1999 article instead. Vishwanath (lesson 11) cites the 1991 piece on the body.
