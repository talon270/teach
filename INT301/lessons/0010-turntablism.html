Turntablism in history

Lessons 5 and 6 told counter-histories of IL. Jean d'Aspremont asks a hard question of all of them: are they really new, or are they new sounds made from the same old records, like a DJ scratching vinyl?

Your win todayExplain turntablism with its three "markers" and test Anghie against it.

1 · Warm-up from lesson 9

Name Charlesworth's two feminist methods.

Searching for silences; world traveling.

2 · The argument

  • The "historical turn" (watershed: Koskenniemi, The Gentle Civilizer of Nations, 2001) is a misnomer. Lawyers were historically conscious since the Enlightenment and 19th-century professionalisation (Oppenheim, Nys, Lauterpacht). What changed was a historiographical appetite.
  • But it is turntablism: new sounds from the same records. The same heroes (Grotius, Vitoria, Lauterpacht: critique still canonises them), the same periods (Westphalia…), and the same markers, periodisation and causal sequencing.
  • This holds even for post-colonial and first-generation TWAIL histories (Alexandrowicz, Elias, Anand), which sought a "truly universal" IL.
  • It is resilient (Hayden White; Koskenniemi's resignation to some Eurocentrism).
  • Cure: radical historical critique: register and consciously ignore the dominant terms, and find "new records". Four obstacles (empirical rigour, language, social and cultural, unintelligibility) are surmountable.

What does d'Aspremont say historians of IL should look for instead of the same old records?

"New records", through radical historical critique.

3 · Use it in an essay

Turntablism is a test you can run on any origins story. Anghie (lesson 5) relocates IL's birth to the colonial encounter, but still centres Vitoria. New record, or a re-spin of the Vitoria record? That question earns marks.

Quiz trap. Koskenniemi: resigned to some Eurocentrism; use old materials innovatively. d'Aspremont: radical critique; new records.
