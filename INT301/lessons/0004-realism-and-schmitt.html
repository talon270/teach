Realism and Schmitt

Positivists find law in sources. Two other camps look elsewhere: realists look at what officials actually decide; Carl Schmitt looks at who decides the exception. Both treat power as central.

Your win todayExplain "law in action", the New Haven School, and Schmitt's case against outlawing war.

1 · Warm-up from lesson 3

Name three tenets of legal positivism, and state the Lotus presumption.

Law identified by sources (form, not content); made by human will; separate from morals. Lotus: what is not prohibited is permitted.

2 · Legal realism (Ratner)

  • American Legal Realism (Yale, Columbia): law is "the output of decisions and behaviour by judges and others". "Law in action", not "law on the books".
  • Realists were anti-formalist, not anti-positivist: law stays a social fact. Rule sceptics; Frank was a fact sceptic.
  • New Haven School (McDougal and Lasswell): IL as "a process of authoritative and controlling decisions" serving human dignity. Critics: it dissolved law into politics and served US policy.
  • Scandinavian realism: Lundstedt called IL "an empty nothing"; Ross. Political realism (Carr, Morgenthau): law is "feeble".
  • Ratner defends the legacy against the charge of being "a handmaiden of policy".

Which school, founded by McDougal and Lasswell, sees IL as authoritative and controlling decisions serving human dignity?

The New Haven School (policy-oriented jurisprudence).

3 · Carl Schmitt (Fassbender)

  • Concepts: friend and foe; the state of exception (the sovereign is who decides it); legitimacy vs legality; protection and obedience. This is decisionism.
  • Life: Weimar's leading constitutional lawyer (Concept of the Political, 1927); joined the Nazi Party in April 1933; brought down by the SS in 1936; interned after 1945, never taught again.
  • Attacked the League as Anglo-French and pacifism as "dangerous and untruthful". Calling wars "police actions" and enemies criminals turns regulated war into "a total international civil war".
  • Grossraum: a German-led great space with non-intervention, citing the Monroe Doctrine. Nomos der Erde (1950): the rise and fall of the jus publicum Europaeum. Also The Partisan (1963).
  • Fassbender: "an antidote to an overdose of liberal optimistic internationalism".
Essay use. Schmitt is the foil to Hathaway & Shapiro (lesson 7): they say outlawing war worked; he says criminalising war makes it total. And to Anghie (lesson 5): his European spatial order is Eurocentric.
